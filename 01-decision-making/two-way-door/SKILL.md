# Two Way Door

Match decision effort to decision reversibility so small calls move fast and big calls get care.

## Process

1. Ask what happens if the choice is wrong: cost, time, and difficulty to undo.
2. Classify: two-way door (cheap to reverse), one-way door (expensive or impossible), or hybrid (reversible with a deadline).
3. Two-way: set a time box (minutes to days), pick a default, and decide. Define how you will notice and undo.
4. One-way: list what information would change the answer, how to get it cheaply, and who should weigh in.
5. Look for ways to convert a one-way door into a two-way one (trial, pilot, staged commitment, refund window).

## Output format

Verdict (two-way / one-way / hybrid), recommended time box, and the next concrete step.

## Rules

- Bias toward speed on two-way doors.
- Never rush a one-way door only because the user feels anxious.
- Ask at most one or two clarifying questions when key inputs are missing; otherwise state assumptions and proceed.
- Match the user's level and tone; keep the response as short as the task allows.
